# INV-012 — The angle each extractor reports for a known orientation

Date: 2026-09-08

## Numbering

This record takes 012, the next free investigation number after `INV-011`. The
reservation at 001 is explained in `REF-002`.

## Question

`REF-006` decision 2 fixes the canon's angle as the one the standard's angle
points in, stored in units of 1/32 degree, and names its own consequence in the
same paragraph: emitting the native format of the extractor already in the
image needs a flip of the y axis and **a rotation of the angle**. For the
rotation it cites `INV-002` R-2, which is reported from outside this repository
and carries status `UNVERIFIED`. So the canon requires a rotation no
measurement here has produced.

`INV-011` did the other half. It measured where each extractor puts a minutia
and found the two in opposite vertical frames. This record asks the remaining
half: **what angle does each extractor report for a feature whose orientation
is known by construction, at the invocation this repository uses?**

## What this record can settle, and what it cannot

A measurement here relates a tool's output to an orientation this record built.
It can establish or refute the rotation `INV-002` R-2 reports, which is what
`D-4` tracks, and it does. F-5 says in what sense: `D-4` is worded as a
rotation from *the standard's* angle, and what is measurable here is one
implementation of the standard.

It **cannot** establish what the standard's angle means physically, which is
`D-3`. That is a question about a document nobody here has read, and no
experiment substitutes for reading it. Nothing below should be read as an
answer to it: `D-3` is open when this record ends, exactly as open as it was
when the record began. A reader who takes `D-4` as settled and `D-3` with it
has taken from this record something it does not contain.

Neither does this record say which convention is right. Neither tool is a
reference for the other, and the constructed orientation is a property of a
generator written here, not of a standard.

## Method

**What was measured against.** The tree at commit `a16f58d` and the image built
from its `Dockerfile`, image id
`sha256:e0ded5a5dcc8f594df58ab904be76605ad26492da834130592091b1348f7c3fc`. The
tools are the ones `MAN-tools.v2` records, at the invocations it freezes:
`mindtct <image.png> <root>` with no flags, so the `.xyt` is in the NIST
internal representation and not the `-m1` one, and `iso-extract <image.pgm>
<template.ist>`, whose declared resolution of 500 and ISO output format are
`REF-012` A and B.

**Every image is synthetic and no corpus image was opened.** The biometric rule
is not merely respected here, it is unexercised: this investigation reads no
fingerprint. Every number below comes from an image generated by the function
quoted in "Reproducing this", and the orientation each number is compared
against is a property of that function.

**Source, and measurement, of the same thing.** Where a source line determines
an answer it is quoted, and the measurement is reported beside it. Where no
source line was found that determines the answer — which is the case for
`iso-extract`, F-3 — the record says so and gives only the measurement.

**Where the source is.** The FingerJetFXOSE files are quoted from the commit
the `Dockerfile` pins, `1726ba08bf7f2137d2f861ac1ae124d5cd355eee`, tree
`e523e7b7ad5ab6d6e8b0a6fd163696f13e4a8b48`, each with its blob id. The NBIS
files are quoted from the builder stage, which unpacks the archive
`MAN-tools.v2` pins by digest, each with its own sha256.

**The sweep.** 36 orientations, 0 to 350 degrees in steps of 10, which covers
the full circle and not a quadrant. A convention that differs from another by a
reflection agrees with it on half the circle, and a quadrant sweep cannot tell
the two apart; the reflection is tested explicitly in F-4. Two families of
image at each orientation, one built to carry ridge endings and one built to
carry ridge bifurcations, nine minutiae in each: **648 measurements per tool**.

**The ridge period is 9 pixels** and it is a parameter of the method, not an
incidental: whether the library accepts a synthetic field at all depends on it.
The last section of this record varies the period over four values and finds
the library refusing half the images at 13. Everything in F-1 to F-7 is at 9,
which is the period `INV-011`'s construction used.

## F-1 — the construction

### The field

One 384 by 384 greyscale image per case. A phase is computed over the whole
plane and the pixel is a cosine of it:

    phase = 2*pi*t/period + sum over the nine dislocations of atan2(...)
    pixel = 128 - 110*cos(phase), clamped to 0..255

with `t` the signed distance across a parallel ridge field at orientation
`phi`, and `period` 9. Ridges are the **dark** level sets, `phase = 0` modulo
`2*pi`; valleys are the light ones. `phi` is measured counterclockwise from the
`+x` axis as the image is displayed, so the unit vector along the ridge is
`u = (cos phi, -sin phi)` in (column, row) and the perpendicular is
`n = (-sin phi, -cos phi)`.

**No pixel is erased.** Each minutia is a phase dislocation: a term
`atan2` about a point, which inserts one extra ridge and terminates a line at
that point. This matters. A first attempt built minutiae by blanking a ridge
over part of its length, and `mindtct` found every one of them while the
library refused every such image with `FRFXLL_ERR_FB_TOO_SMALL_AREA`, which
`FeatureExtraction.h:181` raises when it has found fewer than the
`min_minutia = 6` of `FeatureExtraction.h:46`, blob
`5a775d0ccdceb776211f8ab296f87e401a8fe981`. A blanked ridge is a hole in the
image; a dislocation is a feature of the ridge flow, and both tools find it.

### The nine dislocations, and which type each carries

Nine dislocations on a square lattice, at offsets of -110, 0 and +110 pixels
along `u` and along `n` from the image centre. Each is then shifted across the
ridges until the phase there, computed from the parallel term and the other
eight dislocations, reaches a target: **0 for a ridge ending**, so that the
line terminating at the point is dark, and **pi for a ridge bifurcation**, so
that the terminating line is light and the two ridges beside it merge. The
shift is solved by iteration and the residual is **0.0000 degrees at the worst
of the nine**, in both families.

### The constructed direction

The direction is defined as **the direction, from the minutia, along the line
that survives**. It is computed exactly, from the gradient of the phase with
that dislocation's own singular term removed:

    g = (2*pi/period)*n + sum over the other eight of grad atan2
    direction = the perpendicular of g, taken in the sense of +u

so it is the local ridge direction, and it is not exactly `phi`: the other
eight dislocations tilt the field. Over all 648 points the constructed
direction differs from the nominal `phi` by at most **2.521 degrees**, and
every comparison in this record uses the exact per-point value, never `phi`.

### Verified without either tool

The construction is what this whole record rests on, so it is checked twice by
arithmetic on the generated pixels, with no extractor involved.

- **The axis.** A structure tensor over a square window of half-width 22 about
  each point, with the disc of radius 9 about the point itself removed so that
  the dislocation does not enter it, gives the ridge axis modulo 180 degrees.
  Over 90 points — orientations 0, 30, 90, 180 and 270, nine points, both
  families — it agrees with the analytic direction to within **1.160 degrees**
  at the worst.
- **The sense.** The mean pixel value along the ray from each point in the
  `+u` direction, sampled from one to three periods out, against the same
  along `-u`:

| family | `+u` ray mean | range | `-u` ray mean | range |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ending | 28.5 | 18.0..36.2 | 227.4 | 216.1..238.0 |
| bifurcation | 224.8 | 197.7..238.0 | 29.2 | 18.0..54.1 |

  On the ending family the dark line continues on the `+u` side and is absent
  on the other; on the bifurcation family the light line does. 45 points each.

The structure tensor fixes the axis but not the sense, and the ray test fixes
the sense. Together they establish the constructed direction from the pixels
alone. What neither establishes is that the *feature* at the point is the type
the family name says: that the terminating dark line is what an extractor will
call a ridge ending is the tools' business, and F-6 reports what each called
it rather than assuming.

## F-2 — what `mindtct` reports

### From the source

`/src/mindtct/src/lib/mindtct/results.c`, sha256
`f496195f70105b1d72807c56e975c730e47550b848136a9cf656c7e916546ac6`, lines 123
to 129, above the `.min` writer:

    /* 1. Write Minutiae results to text file "<oroot>.min". */
    /*    XYT's written in LFS native representation:        */
    /*       1. pixel coordinates with origin top-left       */
    /*       2. 11.25 degrees quantized integer orientation  */
    /*          on range [0..31]                             */

It gives the `.min` direction's step and range and **does not say which
direction the index 0 points in, nor whether it points along the ridge or away
from it.** That is why the measurement below is needed for the `.min` file at
all.

`/src/mindtct/src/lib/mindtct/xytreps.c`, sha256
`93a79128b210b9838660f45cdcf3da0fb82c91ae2875da7847d2e9854854de50`, is more
forthcoming about the `.xyt` this repository produces. Lines 86 to 93, in
`lfs2nist_minutia_XYT`, the branch `MAN-tools.v2`'s no-flag invocation takes:

    /*       XYT's according to NIST internal rep:           */
     /*      1. pixel coordinates with origin bottom-left    */
    /*       2. orientation in degrees on range [0..360]     */
    /*          with 0 pointing east and increasing counter  */
    /*          clockwise (same as M1)                       */
    /*       3. direction pointing out and away from the     */
    /*             ridge ending or bifurcation valley        */
    /*             (opposite direction from M1)              */

and lines 98 to 103 are the arithmetic:

    degrees_per_unit = 180 / (float)NUM_DIRECTIONS;

    t = (270 - sround(minutia->direction * degrees_per_unit)) % 360;
    if(t < 0){
       t += 360;
    }

`lfs.h`, sha256
`1944208fdb9c3a24c7d01e0e20584cb407d9a3b5a710f20c942a0664c86c0a71`, line 356
sets `NUM_DIRECTIONS` to **16**, so `degrees_per_unit` is 180/16 = **11.25**,
and line 96 defines

    #define sround(x) ((int) (((x)<0) ? (x)-0.5 : (x)+0.5))

which rounds half away from zero, and for the non-negative product here, half
up.

**Three things follow from those lines and are stated because they are
quotation, not observation.** The `.xyt` angle is degrees, 0 east, increasing
counterclockwise. The direction points **away** from the ridge, and the same
sentence says it points away from the bifurcation *valley*, so the file states
one rule for both types. And the conversion is
`(270 - round_half_up(11.25 d)) mod 360`, which is exactly the third of the
three candidate rules `INV-002` F-7 tested — the one that agreed on 3946 of
3946 minutiae there while the two reported rules did not. `INV-002` recorded
under "what was left unchecked" that the source had not been read and that no
further image could separate that rule from the true one. **The source is read
here and it is that rule.**

### By measurement, the `.xyt` angle

`mindtct` produced exactly 9 minutiae on every one of the 72 images, and each
of the 648 constructed points had one within 6 pixels of it; the largest
pairing distance over all 648 was **2.12 pixels**. Coordinates were compared in
the image's own frame, converting the `.xyt` y by `INV-011` F-1.

Against the constructed direction, over all 648:

| statistic | value |
| --- | --- |
| circular mean of (reported − constructed) | **178.624 degrees** |
| concentration R of that difference | **0.99825** |
| the difference, least and greatest | 171.992 and 185.032 |
| residual after subtracting 180, least and greatest | −8.008 and +5.032 |
| mean residual after subtracting 180 | −1.376 |
| residuals within half a grid step, 5.625 degrees | 560 of 648 |
| reported equals the grid point nearest constructed + 180 | 572 of 648 |

**`mindtct`'s `.xyt` angle points opposite to the ridge the minutia belongs
to.** The residual never leaves one grid step of 180 degrees, and the
measurement is consistent with the offset being exactly 180; it cannot resolve
better than the 11.25-degree grid, and the mean residual of −1.376 is what a
sweep at 10-degree steps against an 11.25-degree grid produces rather than a
property of the tool.

**It is an offset and not a reflection.** Under a reflection the sum
`reported + constructed` would be constant; measured, that sum has
concentration **R = 0.00336** over the 648, which is what a quantity spread
around the whole circle gives. The sweep covering the full circle is what
allows this to be said: on any half of it the two hypotheses agree.

### By measurement, the `.min` direction index

The index took all 32 values, 0 to 31. Against the constructed direction,

    d == round(8 - constructed/11.25) mod 32       576 of 648

with the disagreements the same quantisation slack as above, and at the four
directions that fall on a grid point exactly:

| constructed | 0 | 90 | 180 | 270 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `.min` direction index | 8 | 0 | 24 | 16 |

So the index runs the opposite way round the circle from the display angle, and
`d = 0` is a minutia whose ridge body lies at 90 degrees, straight up the
screen. The relation between the two files
reproduces here as well: `(270 - round_half_up(11.25 d)) mod 360` equals the
`.xyt` angle on **648 of 648**.

## F-3 — what `iso-extract` reports

### What the source says, and what it does not

The theta byte is the fifth of the six bytes of each minutia, at offset
`32 + 6i`, by the layout `INV-011` F-2 derived from `serializeFpData.h`, blob
`cfba519962b3c3b6c65f1dc5a8d0b66f53e889e0`.

Two lines determine what is in it, and neither relates it to the image.
`INV-003` S-1 quotes the ISO serializer, `serializeFpData.h:386-388`, writing
the library's internal 8-bit value **unchanged**, where the ANSI serializer
would scale it by 180/256. `INV-003` S-7 quotes
`FeatureExtraction.h:150-151`, blob
`5a775d0ccdceb776211f8ab296f87e401a8fe981`, applying `theta = -theta + 64` to
every minutia before serialization, and `INV-003` F-3 records what that is —
by S-1's unit of 360/256 degrees, 64 is 90 degrees, so the transform is a
reflection followed by a quarter turn — and records equally that it is defined
against the library's own internal angle, **which no line quoted there or here
relates to any other convention**.

So for this extractor there is no source line to set beside the measurement.
Where the internal angle comes from was not traced, and the paragraph below is
the whole of what this record establishes about `iso-extract`'s angle.

### By measurement

The library accepted all 72 images. It found exactly 9 minutiae in 69 of them
and 10 in three — the `bifurcation` family at 160 degrees and the `ending`
family at 170 and 350 — and each of the 648 constructed points had one within 6
pixels; the largest pairing distance was **4.04 pixels**. The byte was read as
degrees at 360/256 = 1.40625 degrees per step, which is the unit `INV-003` F-1
derives.

| statistic | value |
| --- | --- |
| circular mean of (reported − constructed) | **0.117 degrees** |
| concentration R of that difference | **0.99967** |
| residual, least and greatest | −3.592 and +5.473 degrees |
| the same in byte steps | −2.55 and +3.89 |
| residuals within half a byte step | 261 of 648 |
| byte within 2 of `round(constructed / 1.40625)` | 636 of 648, never more than 4 |

**`iso-extract`'s angle points along the ridge the minutia belongs to, in the
same sense as the constructed direction, and its zero is east with the angle
increasing counterclockwise as the image is displayed.** As with `mindtct` the
sum `reported + constructed` is not constant — **R = 0.00070** — so this too is
an offset, of zero, and not a reflection.

The scatter is wider than the byte step, up to four steps: this extractor's
angle is not a quantisation of the constructed direction the way `mindtct`'s
is, it is an estimate that lands within a few units of it. That is a statement
about the spread of the measurement and not about a convention.

## F-5 — `INV-002` R-2, reproduced

`INV-002` R-2 reports, with status `UNVERIFIED` and from outside this
repository, that ISO angles point about 180 degrees away from native NBIS
angles: a circular mean difference of **183.0** degrees with concentration
**R = 0.97**, over 22 minutiae that the two extractors placed within 6 pixels
of each other on one image.

**It reproduces.** Taken the same way R-2 was taken — the difference between
the two tools' angles at the same minutia — over the 648 constructed points:

| | R-2, reported | here, measured |
| --- | --- | --- |
| circular mean of (ISO − native) | 183.0 degrees | **181.494 degrees** |
| concentration R | 0.97 | **0.99789** |
| sample | 22 minutiae, one image | 648, 72 synthetic images |

The same conclusion follows from F-2 and F-3 without pairing the tools at all,
and that is the stronger form: `mindtct` reports the constructed direction plus
180 and `iso-extract` reports it plus 0, each measured against an orientation
neither tool supplied, so the half turn between them is a consequence of two
separate measurements rather than a statistic about a pairing. F-2's source
quotation says the same thing in words: the `.xyt` direction points *away* from
the ridge.

This is the fact `D-4` tracks, and `REF-006` decision 2's rotation is that half
turn. What to do with `INV-002` R-2's status, and whether `D-4` is closed, are
decisions; `REF-002` keeps history rather than rewriting it, so `INV-002` is
not edited here and no refinement is taken here.

**What it does not settle, and the wording matters.** `D-4` is worded as the
rotation between *the standard's* angle and the native one. What was measured
is the angle this library writes into an ISO template, which is an
implementation of the standard and not the standard: `REF-007`'s note on `D-3`
says that an implementation is not a specification, and that applies here in
full. A converter that emits what this extractor reads stands on measured
ground. A claim about what the standard requires does not, and this record
supplies none. The half turn is between two tools; it says nothing about what
the standard's angle means, which is `D-3`, and nothing about which of the two
directions a reader should call correct.

rule, "out and away from the ridge ending or bifurcation valley". For
`iso-extract` there is no such sentence and the equality above is the whole of
the evidence.

The construction produced bifurcations of known orientation, so nothing is left
unmeasured for want of one. What it did not do is establish independently that
the feature at a `bifurcation`-family point *is* a bifurcation: F-1's ray test
shows that the line terminating there is light and the dark lines beside it
merge, and calling that a bifurcation is what both tools did, not what this

is what a spread of a few units around 36 nominal orientations produces; it is
not a count of the levels the format has.

## Reproducing this

Every command was run on 2026-09-08 against the tree at `a16f58d`. `$SP` is a
scratch directory outside the tree. No corpus image was mounted into any
container and none was read: this record needs none.

**G — the generator, in full.** `phi` is the nominal orientation in degrees,
`period` is 9, and `LATTICE` is the nine offsets
`(a, b)` with `a` and `b` each in `(-110, 0, 110)`.

    def frame(phi_deg):
        phi = math.radians(phi_deg)
        return ((math.cos(phi), -math.sin(phi)),
                (-math.sin(phi), -math.cos(phi)))

    def background_phase(j, ab):
        a, b = ab[j]
        ph = 2.0 * math.pi * b / PERIOD
        for i, (ai, bi) in enumerate(ab):
            if i != j:
                ph += math.atan2(b - bi, a - ai)
        return ph

    def place(target):
        ab = [list(p) for p in LATTICE]
        for _ in range(60):
            for j in range(len(ab)):
                r = wrap(background_phase(j, ab) - target)
                ab[j][1] -= r * PERIOD / (2.0 * math.pi)
        return ab

    def render(phi_deg, ab):
        (ux, uy), (nx, ny) = frame(phi_deg)
        dx = (np.arange(W) - CX)[None, :]
        dy = (np.arange(H) - CY)[:, None]
        s = dx * ux + dy * uy
        t = dx * nx + dy * ny
        ph = 2.0 * np.pi * t / PERIOD
        for (a, b) in ab:
            ph = ph + np.arctan2(t - b, s - a)
        v = 128.0 - 110.0 * np.cos(ph)
        return np.clip(np.rint(v), 0, 255).astype(np.uint8)

    def true_direction(j, ab, phi_deg):
        a, b = ab[j]
        gu, gn = 0.0, 2.0 * math.pi / PERIOD
        for i, (ai, bi) in enumerate(ab):
            if i == j:
                continue
            da, db = a - ai, b - bi
            r2 = da * da + db * db
            gu += -db / r2
            gn += da / r2
        du, dn = (gn, -gu) if gn > 0 else (-gn, gu)
        (ux, uy), (nx, ny) = frame(phi_deg)
        vx, vy = du * ux + dn * nx, du * uy + dn * ny
        return math.degrees(math.atan2(-vy, vx)) % 360.0

    Image.fromarray(render(phi, ab), "L").save(name + ".png")
    Image.fromarray(render(phi, ab), "L").save(name + ".pgm")

## What was left unchecked

- **`D-3`, and it is the first item deliberately.** The text of ISO/IEC
  19794-2:2005 is still unread here. Nothing above says what the standard's
  angle means physically; every angle in this record is related to an
  orientation this record built, or to the other tool. `D-3` is exactly as open
  as it was.
- **Which convention is correct.** Neither tool is a reference for the other,
  and a generator written here is not a standard. That one extractor points
  along the ridge and the other away from it is measured; that either is right
  is not a question this record asks.
- **How the two extractors differ in the minutiae they find.** That is the
  next study and it does not begin here. F-5 compares the two angles at one
  constructed point because reproducing `INV-002` R-2 requires the same
  statistic R-2 used; nothing above compares which minutiae the two found,
  how many, or where, and the counts quoted are stated per tool and never
  against each other.
- **Whether the relation holds on real fingerprints.** Every image here is
  synthetic and made of a cosine of an analytic phase. A relation measured on
  constructed ridges is a relation measured on constructed ridges.
- **One image size, one declared resolution, one lattice.** 384 by 384, 500 dpi
  as `REF-012` A fixes it, and nine dislocations 110 pixels apart. Nothing here
  varies any of the three, and `INV-011` F-6 has already shown that this
  library's output moves with the declared resolution in ways not fully
  explained.
- **Where the library's internal angle is fixed.** F-3 quotes the two lines
  that carry it out of the extractor and states that neither relates it to the
  image. The extractor was not traced, so the measured zero offset has no
  mechanism behind it here.
- **Whether either tool's type label means what the standard means by it.**
  Both labelled every constructed point the way it was built, which is an
  agreement between the two tools and a generator, not between either and a
  definition. The standard is unread; see the first item.
- **The scatter.** `iso-extract`'s angle lands up to four byte steps from the
  constructed direction and `mindtct` reports a value other than the nearest
  grid point on 76 of 648. Neither is explained here; both are reported.
- **The `-m1` branch of `mindtct`.** `xytreps.c` 122-150 was quoted in
  `INV-011` and is not exercised here. No `.xyt` was produced with `-m1`, so
  `INV-002` F-13's relation between the two is not re-measured against a
  constructed orientation.
- **Whether all 256 ISO levels occur.** 157 were seen. F-7 says what that does
  and does not show.
- **The canon's unit.** No angle above was converted into the 1/32 degree of
  `REF-006` decision 2, and no conversion code exists in this repository. This
  record supplies a number a reader will need; it does not write the reader.
